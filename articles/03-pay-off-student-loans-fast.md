# How do you pay off student loans fast?

Student loans feel endless because the balance barely seems to move. It does move — just slowly, if you're only paying the minimum. Here's how to make it move faster.

## Step 1: Know what you actually owe

In Canada, your student loan is usually two loans stapled together, and they don't cost the same:

- **The federal portion charges 0% interest.** The government permanently eliminated interest on Canada Student Loans in April 2023. That part costs you nothing to carry.
- **The provincial portion may charge interest.** It depends on your province. Ontario charges prime + 1% (about 5.7% in late 2026). Some provinces, like B.C. and Manitoba, charge 0%.

Check your NSLSC statement and find the split. If your provincial portion is the only part charging interest, that's the part to attack first.

## Step 2: Attack the highest rate first

This is called the avalanche method: put every extra dollar toward the highest-interest debt, while paying the minimum on the rest.

It works because interest is the only thing making the loan grow. A dollar against a 5.7% loan saves you more than a dollar against a 0% loan — the 0% loan doesn't grow at all while you leave it alone.

## Step 3: Raise the monthly payment — the math is dramatic

Take a $30,000 loan at 5.7% as an example:

| Monthly payment | Time to pay off | Total interest paid |
|---|---|---|
| ~$329 (minimum) | 10 years | ~$9,427 |
| $400 | 7.7 years | ~$7,178 |
| $500 | 5.9 years | ~$5,397 |

Going from the minimum to $500 a month saves about **$4,000 in interest and 4 years of payments**. You don't need a huge raise to make a huge difference — $170 extra a month changes the whole picture.

## Step 4: Make extra payments hit the principal

When you pay extra, tell your lender you want it applied to the principal — the actual balance — not just used to push your next due date forward. Some servicers treat extra payments as advance payments by default, which doesn't shrink the loan as fast.

After any extra payment, check your statement and confirm the balance dropped by the full amount.

## Step 5: Use windfalls and claim the tax credit

Tax refunds, work bonuses, birthday money — send lump sums straight to the highest-rate portion. A $1,000 lump sum against a 5.7% loan saves you far more than $1,000 over the life of the loan, because it also kills all the interest that $1,000 would have generated.

And don't forget the student loan interest tax credit: the interest you pay on government student loans earns you a federal and provincial non-refundable tax credit. Keep your NSLSC interest statement and claim it.

## Step 6: Don't drain your emergency fund to go faster

Paying a loan off fast is good. Paying it off so fast that a flat tire goes on a credit card at 20% is not. Keep a small buffer — even $500 to $1,000 — and then attack the loan.

And if the payments are genuinely unmanageable, don't just stop paying. The Repayment Assistance Plan can lower or pause your payments without damaging your credit the way a missed payment would.

## The bottom line

The fastest way to kill a student loan is a bigger monthly payment aimed at the highest-rate portion. Know your split, pay extra against the part that charges interest, and let the 0% federal portion wait its turn.

*Example numbers assume a $30,000 balance at 5.7% and are illustrative only. Rates change — check your NSLSC statement for your actual rates. Educational only, not financial advice.*
