# How to invest your first $1,000

Your first $1,000 is the hardest money to invest — not because the amount is big, but because it's the first time. Here's the whole process in four steps.

## Step 1: Kill high-interest debt first

If you have credit card debt at 20% interest, paying that off beats any investment. No investment reliably returns 20% a year. Clearing that debt is a guaranteed 20% return, and it comes first.

If your only debt is a student loan at a low rate, that's different — you can invest and pay it down at the same time. High-interest debt is the emergency. Everything else can wait.

## Step 2: Open a TFSA

In Canada, the Tax-Free Savings Account is where your first $1,000 should usually go. Money inside a TFSA grows tax-free — you never pay tax on the growth, and you never pay tax when you take it out.

Check your contribution room in CRA My Account before you deposit. Your room builds up every year you've been a resident over 18, so most people in their twenties have more room than they think.

## Step 3: Buy a broad market ETF

With $1,000, don't try to pick one winning stock. Buy a broad market ETF — a single fund that holds hundreds of companies at once. If one company struggles, the other hundreds carry the weight.

You don't need to research 500 companies. You need one decision: a diversified fund, bought once, left alone.

## Step 4: Automate what comes next

The $1,000 is the start, not the plan. Set up an automatic contribution — $50, $100, whatever fits — every payday. This is where the real growth happens.

Here's why the monthly amount matters more than the first $1,000:

- $1,000 left alone for 50 years at 8% a year grows to about **$47,000**.
- $1,000 plus $100 a month for 30 years at 8% grows to about **$160,000**.

The first $1,000 opens the account. The habit builds the wealth.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Waiting for the perfect moment.** There isn't one. The market goes up and down every year, and the people who wait for a dip usually wait for years.
- **Picking a single stock.** One company can fall 50% and never come back. A broad market fund spreads that risk across hundreds of companies.
- **Checking it every day.** Daily moves are noise. The $1,000 you invest today matters in 10 years, not on Thursday.

## The bottom line

Kill the expensive debt, open a TFSA, buy one diversified fund, and automate the next deposit. That's it. The amount matters less than starting — $1,000 invested beats $0 invested every time.

*All numbers assume an 8% average annual return and are illustrative only — actual returns vary year to year and are never guaranteed. Educational only, not financial advice.*
