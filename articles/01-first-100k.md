# How do you get to your first $100,000?

Charlie Munger said it best: the first $100,000 is the hardest. He was right, and the math shows why.

## Why the first $100,000 is the hardest

Early on, your contributions do almost all the work. Your investments haven't had time to compound yet, so the balance grows mostly because you keep adding money.

Here's what that looks like. Say you invest $500 a month and earn an 8% average annual return:

- It takes about **10.6 years** to reach $100,000.
- Of that $100,000, you contributed about **$63,750**. Investment growth added about **$36,250**.

You did most of the work yourself. That's why it feels slow.

## Then it flips

Keep doing the exact same thing — same $500 a month, same 8% — and the second $100,000 takes only about **5.7 years**. Nearly twice as fast, with no extra effort from you.

That's compounding taking over. At 8%, your money roughly doubles every 9 years (divide 72 by 8 — that's the rule of 72). Once the balance is big enough, the growth on the balance starts doing more than your monthly contributions do.

The first $100,000 is a savings game. Everything after it is a patience game.

## How to actually get there

**1. Automate it on payday.** Pick an amount and have it move the day your pay lands. If you have to decide every month, some months you won't.

**2. Start with what you can, then raise it.** $500 a month gets there in about 10.6 years at 8%. At $1,000 a month, it's about 6.4 years. But $100 a month beats $0 a month — the habit matters more than the starting amount.

**3. Put it somewhere it can grow.** In Canada, a TFSA holding a broad market ETF is the usual starting point: the growth is tax-free, and one fund holds hundreds of companies so you're not betting on a single stock.

**4. Don't stop when the market drops.** A downturn means the same $500 buys more. The people who reach $100,000 aren't the ones who timed it perfectly — they're the ones who kept going when it felt pointless.

## What slows people down

- **Waiting to start.** Every year you wait, the monthly amount needed goes up. Starting beats waiting — that's the whole story of compounding.
- **Trying to pick winners.** Beating the market consistently is rare — about 90% of professional fund managers underperform the S&P 500 over 15 years. Your job isn't to outsmart them. It's to stay invested.
- **Stopping and starting.** Pausing contributions for a year doesn't just cost you that year's deposits. It costs you decades of growth on those deposits.

## The bottom line

The first $100,000 feels like pushing a boulder uphill because, mostly, it is. Your money isn't doing much yet — you are. But once you're past it, the hill starts sloping the other way.

Start with whatever you can, automate it, and let time do the rest.

*All numbers assume an 8% average annual return and are illustrative only — actual returns vary year to year and are never guaranteed. Educational only, not financial advice.*
